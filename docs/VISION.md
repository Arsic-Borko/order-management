# Order Management — Vision

## 1. Purpose

Order Management is an evolving software project intended to serve as the technical foundation for a potential future product.

The project combines practical software engineering with continuous learning, experimentation, and exploration of real-world business problems.

Our goal is not simply to build another CRUD application, but to understand how to design, implement, and evolve a reliable, maintainable, and efficient software system.

## 2. Long-Term Vision

Our long-term ambition is to develop an independent technology product that solves meaningful problems and has the potential to become a sustainable business.

We are particularly interested in intelligent logistics, automation, optimization, and the potential applications of artificial intelligence.

However, no specific market, customer segment, or business model has been validated yet.

The project's direction will evolve through technical experimentation, research, and real-world feedback.

## 3. Engineering Goals

### Software Architecture

Develop a deeper understanding of software architecture through practical implementation and deliberate design decisions.

Key areas include:

- Domain modeling and encapsulation
- Separation of concerns and modularity
- Dependency management
- Maintainability and extensibility
- Architectural trade-offs and decision records
- Reliability and system evolution

### Algorithms & Performance Engineering

Treat algorithmic efficiency as an integral part of software design.

Key areas include:

- Data structures and algorithms
- Time and space complexity (Big O)
- Algorithm selection and trade-offs
- Profiling and benchmarking
- Memory efficiency
- Optimization based on measurable evidence

### Software Engineering Practices

Apply sound engineering principles throughout the development process.

These include automated testing, refactoring, fail-fast validation, Design by Contract, DRY, and continuous improvement.

### Distributed Systems

Gradually explore distributed-system challenges when justified by actual requirements.

Topics may include concurrency, consistency, messaging, fault tolerance, and scalability.

## 4. Potential Product Direction

Order management provides the initial domain for exploring software design and business workflows.

Potential future directions include:

- Delivery and resource scheduling
- Dispatch optimization
- Route planning
- Operational decision support
- Intelligent automation
- AI-assisted logistics

These are exploration areas, not committed product requirements.

The project must remain flexible enough to adapt as new opportunities are identified.

## 5. Guiding Principles

1. **Solve problems before selecting technologies.** Technology choices should follow actual requirements.
2. **Prefer simplicity over unnecessary complexity.** Introduce abstractions and infrastructure when their value is clear.
3. **Protect domain integrity.** Business rules and invariants should remain explicit and enforceable.
4. **Design with algorithmic awareness.** Consider complexity, data structures, and performance implications.
5. **Measure before optimizing.** Use profiling and benchmarks to validate performance assumptions.
6. **Document significant decisions.** Capture architectural trade-offs through Architecture Decision Records.
7. **Learn through implementation.** Connect theoretical knowledge to practical engineering problems.
8. **Validate product assumptions.** Do not confuse technical progress with evidence of market demand.

## 6. Current Position

The project is in an early experimental stage.

Its immediate focus is establishing a solid order-management foundation while developing engineering skills and exploring possible product opportunities.

Commercial viability remains a long-term objective, but premature product commitments and unnecessary architectural complexity should be avoided.
